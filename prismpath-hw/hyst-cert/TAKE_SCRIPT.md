# The take: one shot, under three minutes

Camera on the bench: board, OLED, pot, and room to lift the walker. Slow shutter (1/30 to 1/50),
exposure locked. Speak plainly, move deliberately. The receipts record themselves.

## Pre-position (before the camera rolls)

1. Press BTN0 (exact policy). Turn the pot slowly until the LED flickers green and blue. Leave the
   pot EXACTLY there. That is the knife edge, pre-found so there is no hunting on camera.
2. Press BTN1 (steady policy). The flicker stops, the OLED reads RESIDENT LOW. This is the opening
   frame.
3. Switches both down. Walker on the battery bank, resting. Bridge double-blinking.

## The take

**[0:00] Open on the board.**
> This chip is running a rulebook someone signed. Not code review signed. Cryptographically signed,
> like a passport. Watch what that means.

**[0:15] Press BTN0. The LED starts flickering.**
> One button press just swapped in a different signed rule, replayed from memory inside the chip.
> No computer involved. This rule is exact: the dial is sitting right on its threshold, and the
> sensor noise crosses the line thousands of times a second. The chip refuses to lie about that.

**[0:45] Press BTN1. The flicker stops dead.**
> Same dial. Same position. Different signed law. This rule remembers what band it is in and will
> not leave until the input truly moves. The fix for that flicker was not a filter in a display.
> It is a new rule, signed, proven, and measured in this silicon. The screen and the light read
> the same register. They cannot disagree.

**[1:15] Turn the dial up slowly through both bands, then back down.**
> Every decision also has a worst case timing bound that rides inside the signature. We measured
> it on the pins. It keeps its promise.

**[1:35] Point at the three ESP32s, one at a time: bridge, walker, PC node.**
> These three radios form a small mesh. This one is the FPGA's receiver. This one rides with me.
> And this one is the PC's window into the mesh, out of band, for watching it and managing it.

**[1:50] Flip SW0 up. Pick up the walker.**
> Now the input is not the dial. It is this sensor in my hand, broadcasting over that mesh. The
> chip decodes the radio frames itself, in the fabric, no processor touching the wire.

**[2:05] Shake it gently, then hard, then set it down still. LED and OLED climb to red, settle to green.**
> My motion, through the air, through a signed rulebook, to a decision. And every decision you
> just watched wrote a receipt.

**[2:35] Close on the OLED.**
> The receipt file for this exact take is posted below, timestamped into Bitcoin. You do not have
> to trust me. Verify it.

**[2:50] Cut.**

## After the cut

Leave everything running and tell the session to stop the recorders. The take receipt, the bridge
console log, and the video timestamps line up by wall clock. Flip SW0 back down when done.

## If a beat misfires

Keep rolling or cut and reset, both fine. Reset = BTN1, SW0 down, pot back to the knife edge
(it has not moved), walker at rest. The state machine does not care how many takes you need.
