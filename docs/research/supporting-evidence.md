**Claim:** the live in-kernel policy hot-swap (`netupdate`) is no longer element-wise — it is a true double-buffer with a single-word atomic commit, so no packet can ever observe a torn table mid-swap; proven by a swap-storm that flips banks hundreds of thousands of times while classifying, with zero torn reads on aarch64 AND x86_64. Proof harness `loader netstorm <a.ppt> <b.ppt>`: preload bank 0 = policy A and bank 1 = policy B (two policies that give different verdicts on one probe packet), spin a thread flipping `bank_map` 0↔1 as fast as it can, and on the main thread hammer `BPF_PROG_TEST_RUN` with the probe — every observed verdict must be EXACTLY A's tuple or B's tuple; a third value is a torn read.

**Result:** the verifier accepts the doubled-map bank-offset program (the highest-risk item). A live `netattach` → `netupdate` on loopback flips bank 0→1 and swaps the policy with no detach. The swap-storm: **200,000 evaluations under ~450k–560k concurrent bank flips, TORN=0**, with the verdicts split ~50/50 between the two banks (aarch64 99,697/100,303; x86_64 98,688/101,312) — proving the flipper genuinely raced the reader and the atomicity held anyway. The **124/124 in-kernel conformance is unchanged on both aarch64 and x86_64** (the double-buffer touches `ppt_net` only). **Honest scope:** SKB/generic XDP, single flow, loopback (no traffic path touched); the atomicity guarantee rests on aligned-word load/store atomicity, which x86_64 and aarch64 both provide. The remaining #88 follow-on — a hardware secure-element anti-rollback counter — is unrelated and still open. Provenance: `prismpath-ebpf/ppt_net.bpf.c` (bank machinery), `loader.c` (`write_bank`, `net_update`, `net_storm`), run on gx10 (aarch64) and the x86_64 edge gateway.
