> against PrismPath's frozen conformance gates to earn its row. The lineage is part of the validation:
> primitives hardened in earlier projects, then proven again inside workloads and gates that did not

Each node projects its raw reading to a 0..3 band in plain code, broadcasts `{slot, band}` over ESP-NOW every 200 ms, and holds the latest band from all three slots (one sensed locally, two received). The fused decision is the decidable part: a 320 byte `.ppt` compiled by `gen_fusion_mesh.py` from a three field Markdown flow (`tof_a`, `tof_b`, `arm`) whose edges include the stale-aware escalation rules, evaluated by the byte exact interpreter every substrate certifies; the winning edge is the posture (CRITICAL when `tof_a <= 1 and tof_b <= 1 and arm >= 2`, WARN on a single close or lightly armed sensor, OK otherwise; a slot unheard within 1500 ms reads a STALE sentinel band (8) that the same signed table escalates to TAMPER while armed, or drops to DEGRADED otherwise, so the fleet is fail-operational rather than fail-silent). Two pre-vetted fusion policies are baked (A arms at knob band >= 2, B tightens to >= 3), each tagged with a 32-bit FNV-1a id. Poking one node over USB with `R` makes it coordinator for a two-phase commit: PREPARE broadcasts the target table + id, each follower re-hashes it and checks the id is on its baked allowlist `{A, B}` before staging and ACKing, the coordinator COMMITs only on a two-node quorum, and every node applies the staged rule at its own `local_now + 120 ms`. Hardening was exercised two ways: a node held in reset (dropout), and a UART command that drops a settable percentage of received verdicts (`0`..`9` set 0 to 90%, `X` sets a 99% blackout; the PREPARE/COMMIT control plane is never dropped). Host script `orchestrate_fusion.py` streams and times every node's posture.

**Result:** live and reproducible on the three boards. **Fusion:** with the sensors moved, all three nodes tracked the same three bands and the same posture at every tick, converging within the roughly 80 ms broadcast phase and never disagreeing; the fused transition into CRITICAL landed exactly when both ToF bands fell to 1 or below on all three, matching the table's boundary line for line, LEDs in lockstep. **Coordinated rule swap:** a single `R` drove PREPARE seq=1 target=fusion-B, both followers `PREPARE ok ... staged, ACK`, coordinator `2 ACKs COMMIT`, then all three `FLIP -> fusion policy B` at epoch 1 with a host observed spread of about 2 to 4 ms (3/3), each follower having re-hashed and allowlist-checked the pushed rule before staging. **Fail-operational under node loss:** holding tof-A in reset drove the other two, once its band aged past the 1500 ms window, to read `a=8` (STALE) and, with the knob armed, escalate to TAMPER while still fusing the live rangefinder and the knob (never blank), returning to normal fusion within about 0.2 s of the node coming back. The escalation is decided by the signed table on the sentinel input, not an ad-hoc code path. **Under interference (measured on the fail-operational build):** a full drop sweep with static sensors held 100% OK at 0% and 50% loss, degraded to a mixed DEGRADED/OK/TAMPER at 90%, and at a 99% blackout kept deciding at every tick (DEGRADED 45, TAMPER 27, OK 3, zero silent ticks), recovering to 100% OK when loss cleared. **Escalation boundary (knob sweep with one rangefinder dark):** 375 consecutive fused decisions across 25 band crossings classified with zero mixed results: arm bands 0 and 1 all DEGRADED (119 and 89), arm bands 2 and 3 all TAMPER (61 and 106), the fork sitting exactly on the arm >= 2 threshold. **Rule-swap-changes-the-decision capture:** with the scene held physically constant (a=0 b=1 arm=2), the signed fusion-rule swap A to B (PREPARE, 2 ACKs, COMMIT, 3/3 FLIP in about 0.6 s) moved the fleet CRITICAL to WARN with no sensor moving: the rule changed, the world did not. (An earlier fail-silent build reported `waiting` under loss; superseded.) **Honest scope:** a demonstrator, not a certified sweep: the millisecond spreads are the broadcast and commit cadence, not measured tight bounds; agreement and degradation were read from a short window and line counts, not a long fault matrix; the per node projection is plain code and only the fused decision and the swapped rule are certified tables; the interference is a simulated data-plane drop (esp_random over the received verdicts, control plane exempt), not real RF jamming; and this mesh is unauthenticated ESP-NOW with an FNV-1a allowlist standing in for a signature (#102 lands the Ed25519 gate on the status mesh; porting it to the fusion mesh is the named follow on). Provenance: `prismpath-hw/mesh-fusion/` (`ppt_fusion_mesh.c`, `gen_fusion_mesh.py`, `main/fusion_mesh_table.h`, `orchestrate_fusion.py`); three ESP-WROOM-32 boards, two VL53L0X sensors, one potentiometer; ESP-IDF v5.4.

### #102 — Ed25519-signed policy hot-swap on the ESP-NOW mesh: the FNV allowlist stand-in replaced by a real signature gate with an anti-rollback version floor (August 2026)

**Claim:** the coordinated fleet policy swap of #100 now authorizes each pushed policy with an Ed25519 signature verified on-device, plus a monotonic version floor that rejects rollback and replay. A follower stages a pushed table only if the signature verifies against the baked authority public key AND the version exceeds its persisted floor; the two-phase commit, quorum, and atomic flip are otherwise unchanged. This is the cryptographic gate the FNV-1a id/allowlist explicitly stood in for.

**Method:** `prismpath-hw/mesh/`. `gen_mesh_tables.py` is the fleet authority signer: it signs each policy's `"PPTM1" || key_id || version || len || table` message with an Ed25519 key (private seed `authority_ed25519.key`, gitignored, created on first run) and bakes the 32-byte public key, a per-table 64-byte signature, and a monotonic version (A=1 permissive, B=2 tightened) into `tables.h`. On the node, `ppt_mesh.c` reconstructs that message and verifies it with vendored Monocypher 4.0.2 (`crypto_ed25519_check`, RFC 8032, public domain) against the baked pubkey, and requires the version to exceed an NVS-persisted floor; the PREPARE frame carries the version and 64-byte signature (about 161 bytes for the 84-byte demo table, under the 250-byte ESP-NOW cap). Before flashing, a host cross-check confirmed the Python signer and the vendored Monocypher agree byte for byte (real signatures verify; a tampered table, a corrupted signature, and a version mismatch all fail). Re-cert harness `recert.py`; the main task stack was raised to 8192 for the verify.

**Result:** re-certified live on the three ESP-WROOM-32s. The signed tighten A(v1) -> B(v2) is accepted (signature valid, version fresh), acknowledged by a two-node quorum, and the fleet flips together to B v2 at a host observed spread of 4.5 ms (3/3). The negative matrix all rejects with the fleet held on B: a rollback B -> A (v1 <= floor v2) is refused as `rollback/replay`, a byte-flipped table is refused as `bad Ed25519 signature`, and a corrupted signature is refused as `bad Ed25519 signature`; no unexpected flip occurs. The version floor survived a true overnight power cycle: after all three boards were fully unplugged, both followers still refused a replayed B(v2) as `rollback/replay` against their NVS floor. **Honest scope:** a demonstrator, not a certified sweep: one authority key and two policies; the version floor is a single monotonic u32 in NVS (a `Z` test command clears it for a repeatable run); the negative injections are driven from one node by test commands (`T`/`W`) rather than a distinct rogue radio; and the 4.5 ms spread is the commit-plus-fixed-delay cadence, not a clock-synced bound. The fusion mesh's rule-swap (`mesh-fusion/`, #101) still uses the FNV allowlist; porting this gate there is the named follow-on. Provenance: `prismpath-hw/mesh/` (`gen_mesh_tables.py`, `main/ppt_mesh.c`, `main/tables.h`, `main/monocypher*.{c,h}`, `recert.py`); three ESP-WROOM-32 boards; ESP-IDF v5.4; Monocypher 4.0.2.

### #107 — integrity under interference: the native codec pipeline emitted no wrong event across a 5-hour fault soak (August 2026)

**Claim:** under sustained intermittent-to-severe interference (loss, mid-stream byte corruption, TCP resets, blackouts), the native Facet codec pipeline in Vector never emits a malformed or untraceable event; malformed frames are dropped loudly, and everything delivered is route-correct. **Method:** `integrations/vector/interference/`.
